A fourth test shape for lower bounds in Lebesgue's universal covering problem

Nicolas Guyon (Comptoir IA, France), with AI assistance (Claude, Anthropic) for algorithm design and computation.

August 12, 2026 · reproducible in minutes · code public

Status & purpose. This is reproducible numerical evidence, not a certified bound. We publish it so that groups with certification pipelines (notably the framework of Xie, arXiv:2606.04458) can use it directly: everything needed — shapes, optimal placements, code — is below. If it certifies, the lower bound for the problem improves from 0.833 to ≈ 0.834.

1.Claim

Consider the test-shape method for lower bounds (Brass–Sharifi 2005; Xie 2026): any convex universal cover contains a congruent copy of each chosen shape of diameter 1, hence its area is at least the minimum, over all placements, of the area of the convex hull of their union. Xie certified 0.833 with three shapes: disk, equilateral triangle, regular pentagon (all of diameter 1).

Adding the Reuleaux pentagon of width 1 as a fourth test shape raises this minimum to 0.834574 ± 0.00001 (numerically). The gain over the three-shape minimum (0.833597 in our reproduction) is about 10⁻³ — roughly 100× our measured optimizer noise — and survived 2000 independent restarts.

 Nelder–Mead multi-start (two-stage), shapes discretized by inscribed polygons (safe direction: underestimates). Noise ≈ 10⁻⁵, calibrated by a monotonicity check (a 5-set once scored 10⁻⁵ below its own 4-subset — mathematically impossible, hence measured optimizer error). High values are systematically attacked with mass restarts: the heptagon fell from 0.83519 to 0.83433 under attack; the Reuleaux pentagon held.

3.The optimal configuration (all you need to reproduce)

Conventions: disk of diameter 1 fixed at the origin. Each other shape: rotation θ (counterclockwise) then translation (tx, ty). Triangle: circumradius 1/√3, a vertex at angle π/2. Pentagon: circumradius 1/(2·sin 72°), a vertex at angle π/2. Reuleaux pentagon: unit-radius arcs on that pentagon's vertices. Best placement found:

triangle: θ=4.15340, t=(0.01710, 0.00919)
pentagon: θ=3.31451, t=(−0.01793, −0.00980)
Reuleaux pentagon: θ=0.79980, t=(−0.01779, −0.00999)

Hull area at this placement (fine discretization): 0.8345731. Reproduce with ESSAIS=500 python3 bornes_inf.py xie3+reuleaux5 (minutes on 4 cores).

4.What we offer, what we ask

Offer: the explorer and a working branch-and-bound certifier (certain-point witness polygons; symmetry quotients; it has already certified the 2-shape warm-up disk + Reuleaux triangle ≥ 0.825 over all isometries in 10 s, and is currently running on the 5- and 8-dimensional cases) — all public, ~500 lines of Python total. Ask: if you run a mature certification pipeline (interval arithmetic with outward rounding), this four-shape set is, to our knowledge, the best known candidate for pushing the lower bound past 0.833. Please take it — with or without us. Criticism of the protocol is equally welcome.
